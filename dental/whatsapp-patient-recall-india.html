WhatsApp patient recall for dental clinics in India

Your patients already use WhatsApp every day, and they usually read a short clinic message far sooner than they'd answer an unknown number. Used carefully, it's the most effective recall channel an Indian clinic has. Used carelessly, it gets your number restricted and your patients annoyed. Here's how to get it right.

Published 7 November 2026 · by Advaya AI

WhatsApp Business app vs. the WhatsApp Business Platform

Many clinics start with the free WhatsApp Business app on the reception phone. That's fine for one-to-one chats, but it doesn't work for recall at scale: broadcast lists are limited, there's no proper record of who opted out, and everything lives on one phone.

For recall campaigns you need the WhatsApp Business Platform (the "API"), which you access through a Meta Business Solution Provider (BSP). It gives you approved message templates, a verified clinic name, shared team access and delivery reports.

Three templates that work

Keep them short, personal, and free of clinical detail. Here are examples to adapt (Meta must approve your final wording):

1. Overdue check-up

Hi {{1}}, it's been a while since your last visit at {{2}}. Dr. {{3}} recommends a check-up every six months. Would you like to book a time this week or next? [Book a slot]
Reply STOP to opt out.

2. Unscheduled treatment follow-up

Hi {{1}}, Dr. {{3}} asked us to follow up about your next appointment at {{2}}. We've kept a few slots free this week. Pick one here: [Book a slot] or reply CALL and we'll ring you.
Reply STOP to opt out.

3. Short-notice slot

Hi {{1}}, a slot has just opened at {{2}} tomorrow at {{4}}. Would you like it? Reply YES to take it.
Reply STOP to opt out.

Notice what's missing: no tooth numbers, procedure names, or prices. The patient knows what the plan was. Keeping it out of the message protects their privacy and keeps your templates easy to approve.

A cadence patients won't mind

  1. Message 1 on day 0, sent mid-morning on a weekday.
  2. Message 2 on day 4, a one-line reminder with the same link.
  3. A phone call around day 8 for those who haven't replied, from your team or a voice assistant that only books or passes them on.

Then stop. A patient who hasn't responded after three touches goes back into general recall in a few months. Anyone who replies STOP, or anything like "don't message me", goes on a permanent do-not-contact list.

DPDP Act basics for clinics

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to patient phone numbers and visit histories. The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025 and are being phased in, with most obligations expected from May 2027. In practical terms for recall:

  • Your clinic is the Data Fiduciary. You decide why and how patient data is used, and you're responsible for it.
  • Any agency or software that messages patients for you is a Data Processor. Have a written agreement that limits them to your purpose and requires deletion at the end.
  • Have a clear basis for contacting patients: for example, a line on your registration form that the clinic may contact them about appointments and check-ups.
  • Make opting out easy and honour it everywhere, not only on WhatsApp.
  • Share the minimum: name, number and a recall date is usually enough. Clinical notes don't need to leave your software.

This is a summary for clinic owners, not legal advice. Phase dates and specific duties should be confirmed with a lawyer.

What to measure

Track replies, bookings, and attended visits for patients on your original list, and the treatment value of those visits. Opt-out and block rates matter too: if they climb, your messages are too frequent or too salesy.
